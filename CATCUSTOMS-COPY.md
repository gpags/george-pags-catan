## 3. Tier Names

"Template" and "Bespoke" are warehouse words. Three pairs:

| | Entry tier | Top tier | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **The Collection** | **The Commission** | *Commission* does the pricing work by itself. Everyone already knows a commission costs more and takes longer — you never have to justify the gap. *Collection* implies curation, not "the cheap one." |
| **B** | **Editions** | **Originals** | The most literally accurate pair. An edition is one design drawn many times with a different cat in it; an original is drawn once and never again. Art-print vocabulary that earns the price without adjectives. |
| **C** | **The Series** | **The Sitting** | *Sitting* is the portrait-studio word, and it's a cat pun that never announces itself as one. Warmest of the three, weakest at signalling price. |

**Recommended: A — The Collection / The Commission.**

*Reasoning:* B is more precise but asks the customer to learn a distinction at the exact moment you want them choosing. A is understood instantly by someone who has never bought art, and "commission" pre-sells the entire tattoo-like process before they've read a word about it. Nav reads: **The Collection** · **Commission** · **Refills**.

## 4. Product Page — The Collection ($99)

**Title**

> The Collection — your cat, drawn into one of three designs

**Short description**

> Three designs. One of them gets your cat. Pick the frame you'd actually want in the room, send a couple of photographs, and the illustration is redrawn around your cat's face, colouring and name. Built in wood, scratched on cardboard, replaced from underneath.

**Benefit bullets**

- **Your cat is in the artwork, not printed on a label.** The illustration is redrawn around them — markings, colouring, the shape of the face.
- **Their name is part of the design.** Set into the artwork the way it was drawn to be, not engraved on afterwards.
- **The part that wears out is the part you replace.** Corrugated insert lifts out. Frame doesn't move.
- **Made by two people.** A father who builds the frames and a son who draws every design that goes on one.
- **Sits out, not away.** Scaled and finished to be seen from across a room rather than shoved behind a sofa.
- **Scratching it is the point.** Nothing about the object gets worse when your cat uses it properly.

**Price framing**

> **$99.** One frame, one illustration, one insert to start. Refill inserts are $12 — so the thing you replace costs less than a bag of good treats, and the thing you paid for is still the thing you paid for four years from now.

## 5. Product Page — The Commission ($349)

**Title**

> The Commission — one design, drawn once, for one cat

**Short description**

> A scratcher that has never existed before and won't again. You and George work it out together — what the piece is about, what your cat is doing in it, what surrounds them. It's drawn from nothing, not adapted from anything. Three a month, because that's how many thirty-hour drawings fit in a month.

**Process overview**

> It runs the way a good tattoo runs.
>
> You start with a conversation, not a form. What your cat is like. What you keep noticing about them. What you'd want to be looking at.
>
> George comes back with concepts — rough, honest, deliberately unfinished, because the point is to find the piece rather than approve one. You pick a direction. It gets refined. You say what's wrong with it. It gets refined again.
>
> Only when you're actually happy does it get drawn properly and built. Nobody has ever been talked into signing off on a drawing here, and nobody is going to be.

**Price framing**

> **$349.** Flat, all in — the conversation, the concepts, the revisions, the illustration, the frame, the first insert. There is no deposit, no hourly, and no line item that appears later. Three commissions a month, and one of them takes about a working week to draw.

**$179 variant** *(if you overrule the recommendation — see §16)*

> **$179.** Flat, all in — concepts, revisions, illustration, frame and first insert. Five a month, which is as many as two people can make properly.

## 9. Objection-Handling FAQ

**What if I don't like the sketches?**

> Then they aren't finished. Concepts exist so you can say no to them — that's the entire job of a concept. On a commission we keep going until the drawing is one you actually want, and nothing gets built off a sketch you merely tolerated.

**How long does it take?**

> `[TURNAROUND — see §15]` A Collection piece is built and drawn to order. A commission takes longer, because roughly a working week of drawing goes into one and only three are taken each month. You'll get a real date when you order, not an estimate we quietly extend.

**What if my cat ignores it?**

> Cats have opinions about placement more than they have opinions about scratchers. Put it where they already scratch — beside the sofa arm, across a doorway they use, next to where they sleep — rather than where you'd prefer it lived. `[RETURNS POSITION — see §15]`

**How do refills work?**

> The insert lifts out and a new one drops in. Nothing is glued, nothing needs tools, and the frame and artwork are untouched by the swap. $12 for one, $17 for two, $44 for four kept on hand.

**Will it look exactly like my cat?**

> No — and that's deliberate. *(Then the paragraph from §6, in full.)*

**What if it gets destroyed?**

> The cardboard is *supposed* to get destroyed; that's the part doing the work, and it costs $12 to undo. The frame and the illustration sit outside the wear surface. If something goes wrong with the frame itself, tell us — `[WARRANTY POSITION — see §15]`.

## 16. Two places I overruled the brief

**Bespoke $179 → $349, five slots → three.**
30 working hours + $10 materials at $179 is $5.02/hour net after postage and Stripe fees. A $99 Collection piece is $14.57/hour on the same maths. At $179 the halo product pays a third of what the volume product pays, and five slots consumes 150 hours a month — a full-time month of the illustration time the Collection tier needs. $349 across a scoped 20 hours is $16.75/hour. Three slots is also truer scarcity. The $179 copy variant is in §5.

**50% partner coupon → free keychain + free refill set.**
At $99 with a $10 build, ~$13 postage and a sitter commission, 50% off sells below cost. There is no template price that both clears a halved partner sale and stays meaningfully under the commission tier. The free-gift ladder already in `assets/catalog.js` costs a few dollars, reads as worth thirty, and cannot stack its way into a loss — which matters because `allow_promotion_codes: true` at `api/checkout.js:275` has no server-side stacking guard.
